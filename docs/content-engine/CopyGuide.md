# KansoBooks Copy Guide

**Status:** Active editorial standard for public content.
**Authority:** Applies to KansoBooks.com pages, articles, templates, and content
engine drafts. Subordinate to `docs/KansoBooksManifesto.md`,
`docs/KansoBooksVision.md`, and product/legal truth files.

## Core Reader

Write for a capable small-business owner who is busy, behind, and unsure
whether the books are right.

They are not trying to become an accountant. They are asking:

```text
Are my numbers correct?
Can I do this myself?
What is my business actually doing?
Can I get this done before tax time?
Will I get in trouble if something is wrong or I am audited?
What still needs my attention?
```

The strongest pain is lack of confidence. The owner may have revenue, clients,
payments, subscriptions, payroll contractors, and a full calendar. They need
useful numbers, but bookkeeping feels like one more technical job they do not
have time or training to master.

## Competitive Frame

Full-service bookkeeping companies sell relief: a human team handles the work,
produces financial statements, and helps the owner feel ready for tax time.
That is the emotional bar.

Internal benchmark: paid bookkeeping services can be used as a private quality
bar for relief, done-for-you confidence, and tax-time readiness. Do not mention
specific service brands in public article copy unless the article is explicitly
a sourced comparison of that brand.

KansoBooks should offer a different path to the same emotional outcome:

```text
Books-ready confidence without becoming an accountant.
AI does the heavy cleanup.
Kanso checks the work.
The owner approves the few things only they can know.
The accountant gets a clean package.
```

Do not lead with ownership unless ownership is the article's job. Ownership is a
strategic advantage and a trust mechanism. The core customer promise is
confidence: correct numbers, visible uncertainty, less manual work, and a
clearer path to tax/accountant handoff.

## Voice

Use:

- plain user language before system language
- concrete outputs: P&L, balance sheet, statements, reconciliations, open
  questions, accountant package, year-end package
- time and effort language: busy, behind, cleanup, monthly close, tax season
- confidence language: checked, ready, still needs review, safe to send
- calm respect for accountants and tax professionals
- empathy for fear without amplifying panic

Avoid:

- making ownership the emotional center of every page
- directly comparing KansoBooks to a named bookkeeping service unless the page
  is a dedicated comparison
- product-strategy language in user-facing copy
- abstract phrases like "system shape," "operating priority," or "proof
  posture" unless immediately translated
- shaming the reader for not understanding bookkeeping
- making the reader feel foolish for using cloud software
- implying KansoBooks replaces accountants
- implying KansoBooks provides tax, legal, audit, payroll, sales tax, filing,
  or entity-specific advice

## Strategic Spine

Not every article should repeat the whole thesis. But every article should know
the thesis:

```text
AI prepares the work.
Kanso proves the work.
You approve what becomes true.
The result is a clean package you can understand and send forward.
```

The bigger idea is not "files are good." The bigger idea is that bookkeeping
should stop being a source of dread. Owners should be able to open the month and
see what is done, what is wrong, what needs them, and what is safe to send to an
accountant.

Ownership matters when it supports that promise: the package can be kept,
checked, moved, and reviewed later. It should not sound like the customer is
shopping for an open-format philosophy.

## Emotional Jobs

Good KansoBooks copy helps the reader feel:

- "I know whether my numbers are right."
- "I can see what has been checked."
- "I know what is still missing or uncertain."
- "I can fix the few things that need me."
- "I can send this to my accountant without holding my breath."
- "I can understand my business without becoming a bookkeeper."
- "I am less exposed when tax season comes."

If the copy only explains a concept, it is probably not done. It should lower
fear, reduce uncertainty, save time, or clarify the next action.

## Rewrite Tests

Before publishing, rewrite any sentence that fails these tests:

- Would a real owner say this to another owner?
- Does this answer a confidence, time, correctness, or tax-season fear?
- Does this name an object, action, or decision?
- Does it make the reader less afraid or more able to act?
- Is local-first supporting the user outcome rather than becoming the outcome?
- Could this sentence live on the homepage without embarrassing us?

Bad:

```text
This comparison can prove which system shape better matches your operating
priority.
```

Better:

```text
This page helps you choose which setup gives you more confidence that the
numbers are right, the missing questions are visible, and the accountant has
what they need.
```

Bad:

```text
KansoBooks takes the local-first side because bookkeeping should be portable,
inspectable, and explainable.
```

Better:

```text
KansoBooks takes the local-first side because confidence should not require a
large monthly bookkeeping bill or blind trust in a dashboard.
```

## Article Balance

Each article needs one primary reader job. Add the bigger KansoBooks vision only
when it helps that job.

Good placements:

- opening: one sentence that names the real fear or tradeoff
- table: one row about correctness, tax-time readiness, handoff, or time saved
- proof boundary: what the page can help the reader trust
- next step: what to do in the next 10 minutes

Bad placements:

- repeating the manifesto in every intro
- turning every resource into a SaaS critique
- discussing ownership without tying it to correctness, cost, confidence,
  accountant handoff, or tax-season readiness
- using "proof" without naming what was checked or what is still uncertain

## Publication Gate

The final audit must block or request revision when:

- the page sounds like an internal architecture memo
- the opening does not answer a user fear or practical job
- the page treats ownership as more important than correctness, confidence, or
  getting the work done
- the Kanso vision is absent from a strategic comparison page
- the article uses proof language without naming what evidence, decision, or
  package the user actually sees
- the article is safe but forgettable
