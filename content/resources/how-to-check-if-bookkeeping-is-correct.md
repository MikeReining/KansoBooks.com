You can check whether bookkeeping is probably correct by looking for six things: all source files are included, transactions are complete, balances tie to statements, transfers and duplicates are handled, unusual items are explained, and open questions are visible. If those checks pass, the books are easier to trust. If they do not, the problem should be named before you rely on the reports.

The goal is not to become an accountant. The goal is to stop guessing. A small-business owner should be able to see whether the numbers came from real records, whether obvious mistakes were caught, and what still needs review.

KansoBooks treats correctness as something you can inspect. AI can draft categories and cleanup work, but the record becomes trustworthy only when source files, checks, and owner-approved decisions explain why the numbers are safe to send forward.

## Correctness Checklist

| Check | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Source files are present | Every bank, card, loan, and payment processor account in scope has a statement or export. | The books depend on connected feeds, memory, screenshots, or partial downloads. |
| Transactions are complete | The transaction list matches the source files or missing items are named. | Reports look tidy, but no one checked whether every source made it in. |
| Balances tie out | Ending balances match statements, or the differences are listed. | Categories are done, but balances were never checked. |
| Transfers are handled | Transfers are paired or flagged as unresolved. | Money moving between accounts shows up as income or expense. |
| Duplicates are reviewed | Duplicate-looking imports, deposits, fees, and transfers are removed or explained. | Totals seem high because the same activity appears twice. |
| Unusual items are explained | Large, one-time, owner, loan, refund, personal-looking, or unclear items have notes. | Strange transactions are buried in broad categories. |
| Open questions are visible | Owner questions and accountant questions are listed. | The only record of uncertainty is a feeling that something is off. |

If a report looks polished but fails these checks, it is not ready yet. Clean formatting is not the same as correct books.

## What This Helps You Decide

Use this checklist when you want to know whether the bookkeeping is solid enough to keep working, review, or send forward.

It helps answer three owner questions:

1. Are the numbers based on complete records?
2. Can I see what was checked?
3. What still needs a decision?

The most useful outcome is not a perfect score. It is a short, visible list: what passed, what failed, and what needs the owner or accountant next.

## The 10-Minute Correctness Test

Pick one account, one report total, and one unusual transaction.

In ten minutes, can you show:

| Question | Good answer | Not ready yet |
|---|---|---|
| Where did this account data come from? | A named statement or export. | "The app pulled it in." |
| Does the ending balance match? | The statement balance matches, or the difference is named. | No balance check exists. |
| What makes this report total believable? | The total can be traced to reviewed transactions. | The total is accepted because it looks normal. |
| Why is the unusual item categorized that way? | A note, evidence file, or review decision explains it. | The category was guessed or accepted without review. |
| What still needs help? | A specific owner or accountant question is written down. | Uncertainty is hidden in miscellaneous categories. |

If you can answer those questions for one account, repeat the pattern. If you cannot, the next step is not more reporting. The next step is to make the missing source, mismatch, or unclear decision visible.

## What You Can Prove

This checklist can help show whether bookkeeping work has the basic evidence needed for confidence: source files, transaction coverage, reconciliation status, reviewed categories, transfer and duplicate review, unusual-item notes, and explicit open questions.

It cannot prove a tax filing position, deduction, audit outcome, legal conclusion, payroll treatment, sales tax treatment, or entity-specific decision. Those require professional judgment. It also cannot make AI-drafted bookkeeping true by itself. AI can prepare the work; checked evidence and approval decide what becomes reliable.

## Entity Summary

- Correct bookkeeping: bookkeeping work that is complete enough, checked enough, and explicit enough to rely on for the next review step.
- Source file: a statement, export, receipt, invoice, or document that supports the bookkeeping record.
- Reconciliation status: whether balances tie to source statements or unresolved differences have been named.
- Open question: anything the owner or accountant still needs to decide before relying on the books.
- AI-drafted bookkeeping: bookkeeping work prepared by AI before validation and approval; useful draft, not financial truth.
